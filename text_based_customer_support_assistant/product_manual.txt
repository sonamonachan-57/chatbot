product overview:

Smart internet wifi works by continuously monitoring your network and making real-time adjustments to optimize performance. By collecting data on your usage habits, the system can predict when and where you'll need the strongest signal, ensuring that you always have a fast and reliable connection.

Troubleshooting:

If router is unable to access the Internet, check that the Internet connection is working, and
then check that the wireless router can get an Internet address.
 
    Internet Connection:

First verify that your cable or ADSL modem has a successful connection. The cable or ADSL
modem status lights indicate if the modem connection is successful or not. Check your modem
documentation or call your Internet service provider for help with verifying that the modem has a
successful connection.

    Wireless Router Internet Light is Green or Blinking Green:

If your Internet light is green or blinking green, then you have a good Internet connection, and
your wiring is correct.

     Wireless Router Internet Light Off:

If the Internet light is off, verify that the Ethernet cable is securely connected between the cable or
ADSL modem, and that both the modem and wireless router are turned on.

Safety Information:

   Radiation Level:
      Wi-Fi routers emit low-level non-ionizing radiofrequency (RF) electromagnetic fields (EMF). These levels are far below international human exposure limits.
   Signal Weakness: 
      The signal strength decreases rapidly with distance, meaning exposure drops significantly more than three feet away from the router.
   Children: 
      There is no established evidence that low-level RF from Wi-Fi affects children, but it is generally recommended to place routers away from sleeping areas.
   Recommended Distance:
      For peace of mind, experts recommend keeping the router at least 6-10 feet (approx. 1-2 meters) away from areas where people spend long periods.
